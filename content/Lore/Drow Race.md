**Matriarchal** society dedicated to [[Lolth]]. **Slavers**.
# Reflections of Lolth
Taught they are superior—chosen by [[Lolth]] to endure hardship and dominate lesser beings. Non-drow exist only to serve, be sacrificed, or feed the spiders.

Hate surface elves — descended from traitors who abandoned [[Lolth]] for [[Corellon]]. These kin are weak, cowardly, and undeserving of mercy.

[[Lolth|Lolth's]] influence pervades daily life. Omens, signs, and priestess interpretations shape decisions and hierarchy. Her judgment may strike without warning, and those who fail her tests return as driders. Reward and punishment are personal. Lolth may descend in one of her forms, enacting brutal vengeance. Reinforces her cruelty and absolute rule.
## Rule of Matriarchs
Female drow dominate all aspects of society. Matrons are high priestesses, generals, and merchants—ruling through faith, fear, and finesse.

To rise, a woman must devote herself to [[Lolth]] and survive the politics of elevation. Males valued as consorts and warriors. Remain subordinate.
# Society of Blood and Poison
House is both family and faction, driven by trade, dominance, and devotion. Lesser clans pledge loyalty in hopes of protection or advancement.

Rivalries between houses are fierce. Power is proof of [[Lolth|Lolth's]] favour. Sabotage—through raids, assassination, or slander—is routine. Within houses, ambition breeds betrayal. Kinship is expendable in the pursuit of status.
## Cities Without Sunlight
Masters of the Underdark, shaping cities of danger and artistry. Carve **splendour** from stone.

Sunlight is toxic; nightfall is freedom. Surface **missions** happen only under full cover of **darkness**.
# Nocturnal Raiders
Surface raids are integral to their economy and religion. Humans, dwarves, and surface elves are prized as **slaves**, **sacrifices**, and **spider feed**.

Large-scale invasions crush entire towns; smaller missions steal captives. Tactics range from terror to stealth, often both.

Raids are swift and calculated, with escape tunnels pre-mapped. **Pursuit** is rare—venturing into the Underdark often means death. Only nobles or royals might warrant a **rescue** mission.
# Slaves and Status
While feared as slavers, most drow houses hold slaves in moderation—primarily for status. Public roles showcase their master's dominance; lesser creatures labour in farms, quarries, and construction.

Unfit slaves meet gruesome ends: bait for hunts, feed for spiders, or ritual offerings to [[Lolth]].

Even drow of low rank can verge on slavery. Weak houses **submit** to stronger ones or risk extinction. Ambitious individuals may rise—but at the cost of their family’s strength, as they’re absorbed into more powerful lineages.
# Driders: Lowest of the Low
Failures—cast out by [[Lolth]] for faltering during her divine test or displeasing her in some grave way.

Transformed into half-spider monstrosities, a new drider is exiled. Its house rejects it, and other drow hurl stones and scorn as the dazed creature staggers into the perilous depths of the Underdark. If it’s fortunate, it finds shelter and heals. If not, it becomes prey—perhaps to a roper, carrion crawler, or rival drider.

Despite rumours, driders **do not serve** the drow as guards, elite troops, or suicide squads. They are reviled **outcasts**, forced to endure a life of misery. **Killing one is taboo**—it interrupts [[Lolth|Lolth's]] punishment and risks invoking her wrath.

Over time, some driders become skilled hunters and expert navigators. Though they will never be welcomed back into drow society, they may find purpose elsewhere. Those brave or desperate enough to seek passage through the Underdark may find no better **guide** than a drider who’s survived its horrors for centuries.
