…and Lathander walked among the waking world. Where His feet touched, rivers were born. Where His hands stretched, blossoms unfurled. To the weary, He gave hope. To the broken, He whispered _renew_.

But in the days of splendour, when the sun rose without fear and the fields were gold beneath the heavens, the hearts of mortals grew proud. Kings crowned themselves with iron, not mercy. Priests spoke of power, not light. They turned their eyes from the east and sought glory in their own image.

And as the sun set that day, it did not rise anew. The people cried, “Where is the Morning Star? Where is the Light of First Dawn?” But Lathander, seeing their hearts had hardened, withdrew His radiance from the earth. A stillness fell. No birds sang. No sun rose.

Then, in the thirty-third year of darkness, when nights grew long and frost lingered in the bones of spring, light broke in the east. Not firelight, nor moon-glow, but the First Light reborn.

He spoke not in thunder, but in warmth, “_You who kept vigil, rise. You who mourned the light, behold its return. I am the Day anew, and I have not forgotten you._” To the watchful, He gave peace. To the faithful, He gave joy. To those who feasted in shadow, He gave no wrath—only light too bright to bear. They fled, for the truth of dawn is mercy undeserved.
