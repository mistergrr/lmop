## Possible Uses
- **Blood of Rage** potion – advantage on Strength checks 1 hour, +1 exhaustion after.
- **Hemorrhagic Toxin** – prevents magical healing unless the wound cleaned (A).
- **Anti-Regenerative Agent** – stops creatures like trolls, vampires, or magically enhanced foes from regenerating.
## Risks & Side Effects
Prolonged exposure causes aggression, territorial behaviour, or nightmares of flying and hunting.

# Extract from the Notebook of Master Alchemist Zagglebix Wytooké
_(Several pages are stained dark brown-red.)_

**Specimen**: Blood of the Manticore
**Condition**: Fresh. Highly active.
**Observations**:

The blood refuses stillness. In glass it creeps and beads along the surface, as though unsettled by confinement. When left untouched it does not clot smoothly, but instead forms sharp, thorn-like masses that score the interior of the vial. Agitation—shaking, stirring, or splashing—causes a noticeable rise in temperature and fluidity.

Prolonged handling induces agitation in the handler. I find myself with elevated pulse, impatience, and a strong aversion to delay or restraint. These effects diminish once contact ends, though irritation may linger. Noticeable tiredness after.

Most concerning is the substance’s resistance to repair. Wounds treated with even trace amounts close poorly and leave ragged edges. Natural healing slows markedly. Magical restoration functions, but without its usual completeness, as if the flesh resists being compelled to mend. When poured onto living tissue, the blood leaves dead-edged scars that do not readily regrow.

This substance does not kill directly. Rather, it interferes with recovery itself. Care must be taken in storage and handling.

Ingestion effects unknown and untested as of yet.
