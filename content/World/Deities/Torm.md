Torm, *the True*, patron of paladins and unswerving enemy of corruption and evil, serves the people of Faerûn by exemplifying the chivalric ideal. An 'ascended hero who lived his mortal life in service to a just sovereign. Torm eschews the pretence of his, fellow deities, instead adopting a humble position that he exists to serve the common good and the rule of law as established by honourable mortal rulers.

God of duty, loyalty, righteousness, obedience and, after the [[Spellplague]], law.

By the late 15th century DR, Torm was the war god of courage and self-sacrifice.
## Favoured Weapon
"Duty's Bond" (Greatsword)
## Symbol
Right-handed gauntlet held upright with palm forward.
# Dogma
Salvation may be found through service. "Every failure of duty diminishes Torm and every success adds to his lustre.

Strive to maintain law and order. Obey your masters with alert judgement and anticipation. Stand ever alert against corruption, Strike quickly and forcefully against rot in the hearts of mortals. Bring painful, quick death to traitors. Question unjust laws by suggesting improvement or alternatives, not additional laws.

Your fourfold duties are to faith, family, masters, and all good beings of [[Faerûn]]

# Followers
The Debt of Dereliction states Torm's agents must expend every resource available to eliminate cults of Cyric and Bane, and to work against the insidious Zhentarim.

Additionally, clerics and paladins of Torm stand vigilant against corruption within goodly organisations.

# History
During the Time of Troubles, in order to gain the power necessary to destroy Bane’s avatar, Torm absorbed the souls of all his worshippers in Tantras.
This voluntary soul transference killed the mortals, ending thousands of lives in a matter of moments.

As the city had been for centuries the centre of his religion, with members of the faithful flocking there since word spread of his arrival early in the Avatar Crisis, entire neighbourhoods were left barren of life.

Even though Torm required every ounce of devotional power to destroy his enemy, he could not bring himself to ask children to sacrifice themselves, especially since he knew many of them couldn’t understand the importance of what was happening.

He assured their faithful parents that the children would be cared for — and to this day, those children, ranging in age from 14 to 28, are known as the Martyr’s Progeny.

Many have gone on to enter Torm’s clergy, and some have even exhibited strange powers related to bravery and strength in the years since their parents heeded Torm’s desperate call.
