# Description
**Eight legs**, scaled rough hide, **petrifying** eye beams, and razor-sharp teeth coated in **poison**. Immobilize prey with a look, turning them into stone statues. Petrified prey remain **aware** of their fate and surroundings.

**Raising Young.** Basilisks typically live in mated pairs and raise several younglings. Families of basilisk have been known to travel far from their nest to populated areas in search of prey for their babies.

**Cold-blooded**. Slow when cold.
# Subspecies
Rare subspecies have evolved in mineral and **crystal** environments. Some elements include **amethyst**, **tin**, and even **salt** (idea: pillars of salt).
## Alternate Lore
Basilisks are created when a [[Cockatrice]] sits on a **dragon** egg. The resulting basilisk subspecies corresponds to the type of dragon egg it was.
# Lore
A character knows the following information with a successful **Nature** check.
- **DC 12**: Some legends suggest that a basilisk’s blood can transmute common stones into other material, but this is likely a case of witnesses misinterpreting the magical restoration of previously petrified creatures or body parts.
- **DC 15**: Basilisks can be domesticated and trained. Unsavoury alchemists pay good money to those willing to steal a basilisk youngling from their nest. 
- **DC 18**: Alchemists have discovered that after a basilisk eats petrified flesh, the basilisk’s gullet produces an oil that can be used to restore a petrified victim.
- **DC 20**: For unknown reasons, weasels and ferrets are immune to the basilisk’s stare, and they sometimes sneak into basilisk lairs while a parent is hunting in order to consume eggs or freshly hatched young. 
# Tactics
A stone-eye basilisk tries to affect multiple targets with its petrifying gaze, sometimes waiting in ambush until opponents draw close together.
